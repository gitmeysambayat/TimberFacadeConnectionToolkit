A symmetric bolted timber joint, from force transfer to a testable hypothesis

A reproducible ambient-temperature study of a single-bolt, double-shear timber lap joint. The calculations connect actions, resistance, detailing and movement with a proposed laboratory investigation.

Educational component study. The geometry, actions and products are prescribed examples. No overall connection approval, construction design, physical test, calibrated finite-element model or personal study completion is claimed.

01 / Definition

One transfer station with two shear planes

The central C24 member transfers a tensile force into two identical outer C24 members. Each outer member carries half the force. Balanced load introduction and identical outer members are necessary assumptions for this symmetry. There is one bolt, one transfer station and no fastener-group reduction.

Specified example

  • C24 reference properties: characteristic density 350 kg/m³, mean density 420 kg/m³ and parallel tensile strength 14.5 MPa.
  • One M12 grade 8.8 bolt, assumed tensile strength 800 MPa; smooth shank through both shear planes.
  • Predrilled aligned holes. No friction, pretension or rope-effect contribution.
  • Service class 2 and medium-duration combined loading; selected modification factor 0.8 and partial factor 1.3.

02 / Mechanics

Calculation chain and reference result

Formulae follow the selected first-generation EN 1995-1-1 framework. Reference values and clause locations are recorded below; supplied products and National Annex choices remain separate checks.

The Johansen resistance is calculated per bolt and per shear plane. The factor of two is applied once. The characteristic modes below use zero rope effect. Net tension is checked on the central member under the full force and on each outer member under half the force.

The tensile size factor is conservatively one. Stiffness is a post-seating linear approximation. A prescribed total free-play allowance is added separately; it is not calculated from hole diameter. A 2 mm movement budget is an illustrative study choice, not a Eurocode limit.

Sustained-load movement

For this same-material joint, the selected first-generation creep model doubles the timber deformation factor for connection slip. Service class 2 gives a timber factor of 0.8. The quasi-permanent variable-action factor is prescribed as 0.3 for the study and requires project-specific confirmation.

The instantaneous characteristic variable action remains in the expression; its creep increment is reduced by the quasi-permanent factor. Prescribed free play is added once, without creep amplification. This is a linear model for one balanced joint, not a measured long-term response or a model of load redistribution.

Source locations: SW2 printed pp10, 22, 32–33, 36–38 and 44–45; SW1 pp157–159, Table 5.4 and Eq.5.86; EN 1995-1-1 2.2.3(5), 2.3.2.2(3), 6.1.2, 7.1/Table 7.1, 8.2.2/Eq.8.7, 8.5.1.1/Table 8.4 and 10.4.3. Net tension is a nominal-section check, not a fracture or shear-out model.

03 / Explore

Change one assumption and inspect the consequence

Results here describe the current scenario. The reference result above is fixed. Increasing bolt diameter can improve a resistance expression while making the fixed end distance or washers inadequate.

Diameter changes automatically set the hole to d + 1 mm. End distances and washers stay fixed deliberately, so incompatible detailing remains visible. Material, loading direction and topology are fixed. Load changes do not select a new service class or load duration; the combined-case factor stays at 0.8, and a separate permanent-only case uses 0.6.

04 / Interpretation
What governs, and what the calculation cannot establish
In the baseline, mode j governs the selected Johansen model. The net-section tensile checks are less critical. The parallel shear planes increase total stiffness and resistance relative to one plane; they do not eliminate member splitting, fabrication effects or load imbalance.
The diameter series holds thickness, overlap, end distance and washers fixed and sets the hole to d + 1 mm. The thickness series changes only the two identical outer members. These are controlled analytical comparisons, not optimisation or measured behaviour.
No overall pass. Product certification and bolt shear/fracture resistance; brittle timber splitting, shear-out and block failure; washer bearing and installation effects; actual supports and grips; durability, fatigue, cyclic degradation, connection fire performance and façade interfaces remain unresolved. Minimum distances and nominal net tension do not predict these mechanisms.


05 / Proposed investigation
A small laboratory programme that can challenge the model
Status: proposed only. No specimens have been fabricated or tested. The objective is to compare the predicted mechanism and post-seating stiffness with observations, then identify where the analytical idealisation fails.
Proposed screening matrix, subject to laboratory review

AM12; 50 mm outer members3Does the symmetric model reproduce local slip and the observed failure mechanism?
BM10; Ø11 hole3How does diameter change resistance and post-seating stiffness at fixed member dimensions?
C35 mm outer members; M123Does reducing outer thickness change the limiting embedment/yield mechanism?
Nine tests are a proposed exploratory programme. This sample size is not a basis for declaring characteristic resistance or statistical population coverage. Record density, moisture, defects and actual geometry for every member so variation is interpretable.


Rig and instrumentation
  • Use a balanced clevis or equalising fixture to load the centre member against both outer members. Laboratory staff must review grip design, restraint, capacity and guarding.
  • Measure force with a calibrated load cell. Place paired local displacement transducers on both sides of the joint to distinguish relative slip from rotation and outer-member imbalance.
  • Record actuator displacement separately. It includes fixture and member deformation and must not be substituted directly for local joint slip.
  • Photograph seating, hole ovalisation, cracks, embedment and post-test bolt deformation. Record calibration, sampling and zeroing in the test log.
Procedure and data products
  1. Measure material and as-built dimensions; check alignment and instrument range. Run a pilot to establish an appropriate capacity estimate.
  2. Finalise the loading history, rate and analysis windows against the licensed EN 26891 text and approved laboratory procedure before testing.
  3. Separate seating/free play from post-seating response; calculate local slip, stiffness over declared windows, peak force and observed failure mode.
  4. Compare each specimen with a calculation using its recorded properties. Retain raw data, scripts and photographs, including anomalous tests.


EN 26891 provides the relevant strength/deformation test framework. EN 12512 is a separate reference for any later cyclic programme. Their public BSI scope pages were checked; the full loading procedures have not been reproduced or represented as implemented.
Numerical follow-up
Use the present analytical results as a baseline, not synthetic experimental data. A later Abaqus study would require an explicit timber constitutive model, contact, bolt representation, mesh assessment and calibration/validation split. No Abaqus input model or solver job is part of this case.


06 / Assembly and wider performance
Manufacturing, disassembly and sustainability evidence to collect
  1. Prepare coordinated drawings identifying grain, dimensions, loaded free ends, hole alignment, shank position and accessible head/nut locations. Confirm actual timber grade, bolt/washer certificates and environmental exposure.
  2. Fabricate a trial detail under workshop supervision. Inspect drilling damage, fit, washer seating and balanced assembly. Confirm the tightening procedure without taking unverified friction credit.
  3. Record assembly time, tools, access and defects. After disassembly, inspect holes, cracks and bolt straightness; assess any reuse proposal from measured condition and appropriate testing.
A proposed coupon inventory uses one 100 × 200 × 600 mm central piece and two 50 × 200 × 600 mm outer pieces: 0.024 m³ of nominal timber before waste. The 600 mm lengths are planning assumptions, not prescribed standard specimen dimensions. Grips and boundary conditions still need design. The 35 mm outer-member variant uses 0.0204 m³. This geometric comparison is not a carbon result.
For an environmental comparison, obtain product-specific environmental declarations, actual mass, transport, machining waste, fixings and an agreed life-cycle boundary. Reuse and demountability benefits need evidence. No life-cycle assessment or quantified environmental saving is claimed.
For renovation panels, the joint must also be coordinated with insulation, sheathing, moisture control and the existing-building load path. Fire exposure, protection continuity and connection performance need a separate assembly assessment. The toolkit's isolated-member section-loss illustration cannot provide that assessment.


07 / Personal learning record
Reproduce the reasoning before using it as interview evidence
The files and code were prepared with AI assistance. Personal review and reproduction have not been recorded. The following exercises turn the artifact into demonstrable understanding.
  1. Recalculate all four baseline Johansen modes and explain why the resistance is multiplied by two once.
  2. Sketch the full and half-force load paths and distinguish this single station from two transfer stations in series.
  3. Explain the difference between instantaneous deformation, sustained-load creep and prescribed free play.
  4. Explain why a larger bolt can fail a detailing check and why a nominal component utilisation below one is insufficient for approval.
  5. Explain which proposed measurements could falsify the symmetry, stiffness or governing-mode assumptions.
Record the date, calculation and any discrepancies only after completing each exercise. An empty learning record does not imply completed study.
